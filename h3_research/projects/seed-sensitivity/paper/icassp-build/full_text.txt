Seed Sensitivity of Composite Video-Quality Metrics:
Three-Model Evidence on the MiniMax-H3
Leaderboard
Anonymous
Anonymous Aﬀiliation
Abstract—Composite automatic metrics decide leaderboard
positions for text-to-video models, yet their sensitivity to the
generator seed is rarely quantified. We reproduce the released
Sci-VBench VT results for MiniMax-H3 to ∼10−6 and then
generate 16 prompts × 12 seeds for three architecturally
different models (MiniMax-H3, Wan2.1-Fun, LTX-Video-2B;
576 videos) under the released protocol. The within-prompt
across-seed SD of VT is 0.051 for H3, so the paired SE of
a single-seed two-model mean difference ( ±0.035 at 95%) is
5.3× the 0.0066 median gap between adjacent released models.
The binary dynamic-degree (DD) component flips in 27.5%
of H3 seed pairs (CI [24.9, 30.2]%), and in model-specific but
always substantial rates on Wan (17.2%) and LTX (11.9%); the
rate is invariant to any binarization threshold in [0.05, 0.60].
Prompt and seed variance shares are nearly equal in all three
models (ANOV A ICCprompt 0.46–0.56). A descriptive join with
2,392 human expert scores shows pooled rank correlation
−0.04 between VT and expert LPF (cluster-bootstrap CI
[−0.09, +0.01]). Single-seed leaderboard comparisons are not
resolvable at released gaps; we recommend multi-seed reporting
and seed-resolved releases.
I. Introduction
Leaderboards steer model development only if score
differences exceed the noise floor of the evaluation pipeline.
For text-to-video generation, suites such as VBench,
VBench++, EvalCrafter and VideoPhy aggregate auto-
matic metrics into composite scores; Sci-VBench targets
scientific prompts and publishes a VT leaderboard over
1,253 videos, where MiniMax-H3 ranks 4th of 8 models
with full coverage. Prior reproducibility work documents
variance-driven instability in ML benchmarks, in NLG
metrics (ReproGen, QRA++), and emerging reliability
concerns for text-to-video metrics; RL evaluation estab-
lished the importance of seed-resolved reporting. What is
missing is a seed-resolved, per-metric decomposition on a
released, fully reproducible video leaderboard: how much
of a leaderboard gap is evaluation noise? The question
is practical: leaderboards drive adoption and research
directions, and a gap smaller than the seed noise floor
cannot support the conclusions drawn from it. Because
released score sets and pinned evaluator stacks exist
for Sci-VBench, the question can be answered by direct
experiment rather than extrapolation.
We answer with a verifiable reproduction and a three-
model seed experiment. Contributions: (1) a numerical
reproduction ( ∼10−6) of the released 150-video H3 VT
results from a pinned evaluator; (2) a seed-power study:
16 prompts × 12 seeds on H3 (192 videos) showing
the single-seed paired noise floor ( ±0.035) is 5.3 × the
median adjacent-model gap; (3) cross-model replications
on Wan2.1-Fun and LTX-Video-2B under matched pro-
tocols, showing the noise floor and rank instability are
properties of the metric pipeline, not of one generator; (4)
a resolution analysis validated empirically, with seeds-to-
resolve estimates for every released gap.
II. Setup
Evaluator. Sci-VBench VT runner at pinned commit
e8cb3988: six metrics (subject/background consistency,
motion smoothness, dynamic degree DD, aesthetic quality,
imaging quality), unweighted mean = VT; one H100.
Human analyses use the released expert dimensions (LPF
= Low-level Perceptual Fidelity, 1–5). H3 protocol. The
released t2v service (task t2va, 768 short-edge, 16:9,
5 s ≈ 5.175 s). Wan. Wan2.1-Fun-V1.1-1.3B-InP via the
oﬀicial inference script: 832 ×480, 81 frames @16 fps, 50
steps, TeaCache 0.10. LTX. LTX-Video-2B via diffusers
LTXPipeline: 832×480, 81 frames @16 fps, 40 steps, CFG
3.0. Prompts. The 16 Sci-VBench pilot prompts spanning
five domains (biology, chemistry, physics, geography, as-
tronomy), verbatim as released. Seeds 0–11; every video
stored with request, checksum, and per-video JSON scores
(all released with the paper).
III. Reproduction (E2)
Re-evaluating the released 150 H3 test-mini videos
reproduces released scores with median absolute per-
metric deltas: SC 1.5e−8, BC 2.4e−5, MS 1.8e−6, AQ
6.2e−6, IQ 1.6e−5, and DD exact in 149/150; median
|∆|VT = 6.2e−6 and aggregate VT 0.735706 on both sides.
The gate passed; every number below uses this stack.
Two determinism facts anchor the chain: the evaluator
is deterministic given a video (10 −6-level agreement with
released scores), and our own generation pipeline is bit-
level reproducible — re-running the LTX runner with
the same seeds regenerates byte-identical MP4s (MD5
verified).

VII. Resolution analysis
Adjacent-model gaps sit far below the noise floor of
single-seed comparisons: the median gap (0.0066) is 5.3 ×
smaller than the ±0.035 single-seed paired 95% half-width.
In a paired comparison over the same P =16 prompts,
the paired-difference SE is σsp
√
2/(P s) (σsp: 0.051 H3,
0.041 Wan, 0.040 LTX). Even with 6 seeds the 95% half-
width still exceeds the median gap; resolving it requires
∼26 (H3) / ∼17 (Wan) / ∼16 (LTX) paired seeds, and
the minimum gap (0.003) requires proportionally more.
The i.i.d.-noise model is empirically checked with subset-
based SEs at s∈{2, 3, 4, 6}: 0.66–1.23× the prediction for
H3/Wan and 1.31–1.70× for LTX — where the model errs
it errs optimistic, so the conclusions are conservative.
VIII. Discussion and limitations
Seed noise in composite video metrics is (i) large relative
to released leaderboard gaps, (ii) dominated by the binary
DD component, (iii) rank-destabilising at prompt level,
and (iv) not averaged away by more seeds. Concretely,
we recommend: (i) leaderboard papers should release
per-seed scores (or at least within-prompt across-seed
SDs) alongside means; (ii) cross-model claims should use
paired same-prompt designs and report the paired SE;
(iii) when only binary components are available, their flip
rate across seeds should be reported, as it captures the
===== PAGE 3 =====
dominant instability; (iv) rankings should not be quoted
to more precision than the seed noise supports. Treating
adjacent-model gaps as unresolved until paired variance
is reported costs nothing and removes an entire class
of spurious conclusions. Limitations: 16 pilot prompts
spanning five scientific domains; one evaluator stack; three
open models (2B-scale Wan/LTX vs. H3), proprietary
frontier generators untested; Wan runs under TeaCache
caching; the human join is pooled and descriptive; the
i.i.d. noise model is approximate and model-dependent (it
errs optimistic on LTX, making our resolution conclusions
conservative). The conclusions are statements about the
Sci-VBench VT leaderboard and the studied models, not
universal laws of video evaluation; the protocol, however,
is fully released and can be rerun on any model family.
IX. Conclusion
A fully reproducible chain — released scores to ∼10−6,
576 seed-resolved videos, every printed number recom-
putable from released per-video scores — shows that
single-seed composite-metric comparisons of text-to-video
models are not resolvable at released leaderboard gaps.
